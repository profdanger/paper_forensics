DNA ratcheting,
one nucleotide at a time.

A critical evaluation of automated forward and reverse DNA motion in a nanopore, and what survived fourteen years later.

Cherf, Lieberman, Rashid, Lam, Karplus & Akeson · Nature Biotechnology 30, 344–348 (2012) · DOI: 10.1038/nbt.2147
Executive verdict
A convincing enabling-methods paper, not a complete sequencing demonstration.

The breakthrough was an ingenious biochemical activation strategy: protected phi29 polymerase–DNA complexes could be captured by an α-hemolysin nanopore and then autonomously advance the DNA, without active voltage reversal. The principle endured; the exact implementation was not the final commercial endpoint.

Mechanism

The capture-triggered molecular ratchet

The blocking oligomer acts as a molecular safety catch: it protects the complex from catalysis in solution but is removed when the complex is captured and electrically loaded at the pore.

01

Protect

Preassemble DNA–phi29 complex with a blocking oligomer.

02

Capture

Apply a constant-polarity voltage to attract the complex to α-HL.

03

Unzip

Electrical force causes forward template motion and blocking-strand displacement.

04

Activate

The exposed primer becomes available for polymerization.

05

Reverse

Phi29 replication drives the template in the opposite direction.

Mechanistic caveat: The exact route of the unzipped blocking oligomer through the protein assembly is a model, not unequivocally directly observed (original Figure 2).

Sanity check: 40/2.5 = 16-fold asymmetry in median transport rate. At 130 molecules/hour, the average completed-event spacing is ~27.7 seconds. Molecular capture, unzipping, stalling, and release matter to overall throughput.

Scientific and commercial relevance

NABC — at the time of publication

Need

Uncontrolled electrophoresis was too fast for sufficiently informative nucleotide measurements.

Approach

Phi29 polymerase + α-hemolysin pore + protection from an engineered blocking oligomer.

Benefits

Autonomous capture-triggered activation, controlled forward/reverse motion, and hours of preparation stability.

Competition

Uncontrolled transport; earlier T7 and phi29 nanopore schemes; electronic voltage feedback and reversal.

Historical evidence

What stood the test of time?

2010

Lieberman et al. establish earlier processive phi29-controlled replication in a nanopore.

2012

Cherf et al. introduce autonomous capture-triggered forward and reverse ratcheting.

2012

Manrao et al. use phi29 plus MspA for sequence-dependent nanopore readout.

2014

Laszlo et al. demonstrate long reads of natural DNA.

2026

Motor-controlled nanopore sequencing is established; the exact original architecture was not adopted wholesale.

Survival verdict: Broad enzyme-controlled transport is strongly established. The precise original molecular activation geometry and its generalized error performance are less independently verified.

Questions for expert discussion

Ten critical questions

1. What proves the activation geometry?

Can distinct complex configurations yield indistinguishable current traces?

2. Physical motion or detection threshold?

How do registry-error rates depend on bandwidth and the 3 ms cutoff?

3. How general is 0.5 nm control?

What fraction of positions resolve without engineered reporters?

4. What is the full processing funnel?

How many captured events complete both phases?

5. What limits the overall throughput?

Capture, unzipping, catalysis, stalling, dissociation, or recovery?

6. Can a single molecule be read repeatedly?

Does bidirectional movement translate into repeated register-accurate measurements?

7. How much event-selection bias exists?

Are complete, recognizable traces atypical?

8. How robust is the system to sequence?

What happens with long, structured, or GC-rich templates?

9. Why phi29 rather than other motors?

Can helicases deliver better uniformity and capture kinetics?

10. What was truly indispensable?

The enzyme, blocking oligomer, capture trigger, or general control architecture?

Sources and limits

Evidence ledger

This review is independent commentary, not a new replication experiment. The original five-page article was reviewed, but its supplementary figures, full methods and raw traces were not comprehensively audited. Follow-up studies support the principle better than independent replication of every original detail.
